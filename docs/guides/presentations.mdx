title: "Presentations"
description: "Slides that hold anything a note can: quizzes, apps, videos, diagrams and canvases."

A presentation is a deck of slides. Each slide holds ordinary note blocks, so a quiz, a slider widget, a custom app, a Manim video, a mind map, maths or a mermaid diagram goes on a slide the same way it goes in a note, and stays live while you present.

## Make one

- **From scratch:** **Presentations** in the sidebar, then **New presentation**.
- **From a note, project or notebook:** the presentation button in its header, **Make presentation**. You choose where slides break (at each heading, or at each `---` divider), whether paragraphs move to the presenter notes, and whether each block gets its own slide. The preview shows every slide before anything is made.
  - A **note** becomes a title slide, then a slide per section: text on the left, its block on the right.
  - A **project** adds a slide for each frame or figure on its canvas, zoomed to it.
  - A **notebook** becomes a section per item, in the order the notebook lists them.
- **Let AI design it:** the third choice in Make presentation. The AI plans the talk: a title, sections, a few short points on each slide, your quizzes, apps and videos on slides of their own, and what to say in the presenter notes, all from what you wrote. Your blocks are used exactly as they are, and any it doesn't place still get a slide. It uses your AI allowance and takes about half a minute.

The original never changes. The presentation is a copy that remembers where it came from.

## Edit slides

Click into a slide and type. The `/` menu has every note block. On the right:

- **Layout:** Title, Section, Text, Text and block (text left, block right), One big block, Blank. Slides made from a canvas use the Canvas layout.
- **On each click:** turn on any block or point to reveal it one click at a time, and pick how it comes in: Appear, Fade in, Rise, Wipe or Highlight. Arrows set the order.
- **Transition into this slide:** None, Fade, Push, Zoom or **Morph**. Morph moves blocks that are on both slides (the same title, the same formula) from their old place to the new one.
- **Skip when presenting:** keeps a backup slide in the deck without showing it.

Under the slide, **Presenter notes** are for you alone: they show in the presenter view and never on a published presentation. At the bottom of the panel, the presentation's **Theme** (Paper, Ink, Chalkboard), **Shape** (16:9 or 4:3) and default **Transition**.

Drag slides in the strip to reorder them; its menu duplicates, skips or deletes a slide.

## Present

**Present** starts from the first slide, **From here** from the one you're on.

| Key | Does |
| --- | --- |
| → ↓ Space Page Down | Next click, or next slide |
| ← ↑ Page Up | Back |
| Home / End | First / last slide |
| A number, then Enter | Go to that slide (hidden ones too) |
| B or . | Black screen |
| Esc | End |

Clickers work too, and you can swipe on a touch screen. Blocks stay live: run the quiz, drag the slider, play the video in front of the room.

**Presenter view** opens your own window with the slide as the room sees it, what comes next, your notes (A− / A+ to resize), a timer and a list of slides to jump to. Put it on your laptop and the presentation on the projector.

## Share and publish

Share a presentation with people, by link, or publish it, as you would a note. A published presentation lets anyone go through it at their own pace, one click at a time, with every block live, or present it full screen. Presentations in a published notebook are published with it.

A slide that shows a canvas carries its own copy of the canvas, so publishing the presentation never makes the original canvas public.

## PDF and embedding

From the **⋯** menu in the editor:

- **Save as PDF** opens every slide, fully built, one per page; your browser's print dialog saves it as a PDF. **PDF with presenter notes** puts your notes under each slide, for your own copy. Readers of a published presentation get a **PDF** button too (without your notes).
- **Copy embed code** copies an `<iframe>` for a published presentation: put it on your course site or blog and readers click through it there, blocks and all. Add `?slide=3` to the link to open on a slide.

## From the CLI and agents

```bash
octonote deck from pag_…                 # make one from a note (or prj_… / ntb_…)
octonote deck from pag_… --ai            # let AI design it
octonote deck create "Talk" --file talk.md
octonote deck show dck_…                 # an outline of every slide
```

In `talk.md`, a line of `---` starts a new slide and a line of `???` starts that slide's presenter notes. See the [CLI reference](/cli/reference/presentations).
